## 3. Market data, Bali 2026

Verified October 2026. Sources in Section 10.

**Pricing**
- Median sold price, all types: **$299,000**, stable through Q3 2025 after an earlier 5% correction
- Median leasehold asking price, 25+ year terms: **$300,000** (3,018 villas analysed)
- Median freehold asking: **$430,000** (1,884 villas)
- Leasehold 10th to 90th percentile: $179,000 to $800,000. Freehold: $400,000 to $1,500,000
- Freehold premium over leasehold ranges 0% (Nusa Dua) to 82% (Pecatu), by area

**What actually trades**
- **2BR is the most actively traded segment at 32.8% of all sales**, priced $209K to $300K by area
- 3BR leads supply at 3,699 units, 2BR at 3,594. The market builds what sells
- Listing-to-sold gap 13.2% overall, 8.3% for apartments. Buyers achieving roughly 6% off asking

**Geography**
- Canggu corridor (Canggu, Pererenan, Berawa, Umalas): **33.5% of all transactions**, now ahead of Uluwatu
- Uluwatu-Nusa Dua: 21.8% to 28.2% depending on source
- Mengwi: 17.9%, best value-to-growth ratio
- These three corridors are roughly 75% of the development pipeline
- **Ubud is not in the top three.** 4th by listing count (219 in the Propertia sample), median $320K

**Supply**
- Villas 87% of supply, apartments 13%
- Leasehold 81.8%, freehold 18.2%
- 76% of villa inventory completed, 24% off-plan. Apartments inverse: 68% off-plan
- 86% of leasehold listings have 21 to 40 years remaining, median 27 years. Only 4% under 15 years
- Villa stock grew roughly 20% per year 2018 to 2023. 39,000+ active short-term rentals on the island
- **11,751 villas currently for sale** across agencies. 16,000+ active listings total

**Demand**
- 7.05M foreign tourist arrivals in 2025, up 9.7% on 2024. Demand is not the problem
- Buyer mix: **Australians 29%**, Indonesians (mostly Jakarta) 21%, Europeans ~20%, Americans 15%, Korean and Chinese 8% combined
- Badung regency foreign demand up 92% versus 2022

**Yields**
- Short-term rental in tourism areas: 10% to 18% gross, quoted
- Blended across property types: ~8.5% gross
- Long-term rental: 4.5% to 7% gross
- Top Uluwatu villas: occupancy above 83%

## 4. The gap

Three independent sources converge on the same finding: **the market has bifurcated into quality versus generic, and buyers have been burned by projections that did not materialise.**

- "The days of any villa in any location generating automatic returns are over."
- Exotiq names the number one buyer mistake as "buying on projected ROI rather than verified structure."
- Buyers now achieve roughly 6% off asking. Price discovery is tightening.

**So the scarce resource in Bali is not inventory, not leads, and not tourists. It is credible numbers.**

11,751 villas for sale. 25+ agencies. Largely the same stock, since there is no MLS and no exclusivity culture. Every agency quotes gross yield on projected occupancy, and buyers have learned to discount it.

**This is the gap BSH is uniquely positioned to fill, and the July strategy doc buried it.** Section 3 of that document lists "real, audited occupancy and revenue data across 538 live villas" as a *secondary point, worth a mention but not the lead*.

That is backwards. Every other element of the stated positioning (best listings, warm owner base, combined sales and management) is either replicable or already being done by four named competitors. Operator-verified performance data is the only element no competitor can copy at BSH's scale.

## 6. The BSH asset, assessed honestly

What the 538-unit book actually is, per Matthieu 2026-10-14:

- **80% is in Ubud**
- **Same developer throughout: Magnitude. All villas share the same design**
- Range 1BR to 5-6BR. All valued under $1M
- **Average occupancy around 60%**, varying by location
- Full data access confirmed. No obstacle to using operating data
- BSH will sell externally managed and externally sourced villas too, not only its own book

### What this breaks

**1. The data moat is narrower than it first appeared.**
- Ubud sits outside the three corridors carrying 75% of transactions
- One developer, one design, means it is not a market-representative dataset. It is deep knowledge of one product in one secondary area
- Ubud is structurally different: strong long-stay and wellness demand, more seasonal short-term occupancy. The 60% figure reflects that

**2. Channel 1 is not the engine the July plan assumes.**
- That plan models 8 to 10% of 538 converting to listings within 12 months, so 43 to 54 listings
- Those listings are mostly Ubud, mostly one design, in a slower-moving sub-market. Sell-through will lag the Canggu corridor
- Reclassify: Channel 1 is a reliable niche, not the growth story

**3. Magnitude carries brand risk that has already surfaced.**
- The July doc records client feedback linking BSH to Magnitude on quality perception, and that is explicitly why Frans leaned toward a separate brand
- If 80% of the book is Magnitude, "we manage these villas" may work against BSH in a buyer conversation. Test before it becomes the pitch

**4. 60% occupancy is not a number to lead with**, against a market advertising 10% to 18% gross and top Uluwatu villas running 83%+.

## 7. Model options, scored

### Option 1. Classical generalist (the Bali Exception model)

Sell any villa, compete on service and reach.

- Enters the most crowded part of the market with no sales reputation and no brand
- Volume model requires volume headcount, 12+ agents, directly contradicting the 3 to 4 closer plan
- No moat
- **Verdict: reject**

### Option 2. Luxury only

- Exotiq (20+ years), Kibarer, Propertia already own this. Luxury is reputation-led and BSH starts at zero
- Fatal flaw: the 538 managed villas are mid-market short-term rental stock, all under $1M. **The owner base does not feed a luxury book**
- Abandons the only differentiated asset to compete where BSH is weakest
- **Verdict: reject**

### Option 3. OLC model, 4 to 5 exclusive developer projects

- Genuinely viable. Exclusivity solves the identical-inventory problem. Small team works. High control
- But Legion already runs this at scale, 40+ staff and ~600 sold in 3.5 years. BSH would be second
- Slow start. Winning developer exclusives takes months against a 90-day clock
- Off-plan is precisely where buyers have been burned on projected ROI. Selling the distrusted thing with no track record
- **Verdict: keep as Channel 2, not as the core model**

### Option 4. Performance-verified brokerage, with BSH as underwriter

Not previously named. The recommendation, subject to Section 8.

**The reframe:** stop selling the portfolio, sell the underwriting.

The asset is not "we hold data on 538 villas." It is **"we are the operator, so we know the real cost stack and realistic occupancy on any villa, including ones we do not manage."**

- Every agency in Bali quotes gross yield on projected occupancy
- BSH can quote **net**, including the lines nobody shows: management fee, OTA commission, maintenance reserve, staffing, tax
- Because BSH takes over management after the sale, the projection becomes a commitment rather than a marketing claim

**Use the 60% as the weapon, not the weakness:**

> "The market quotes you 15% gross. We run 538 villas. Our book averages 60% occupancy. Here is what that actually nets, and here is what we will sign up to deliver."

In a market where buyers have been burned on projections, the agency that tells an uncomfortable truth and backs it with operator data wins on trust. It is a positioning play directly into the gap: everyone else inflates, BSH verifies. Critically, **it works on externally managed and externally sourced villas**, which is what BSH will actually be selling.

- **Buyer:** yield buyer, $250K to $600K, the 2BR and 3BR band that actually trades
- **Team:** small and specialised. 3 to 4 closers, one listing/sourcing agent, inbound-led. A data-led pitch converts better and needs fewer bodies
- **Flywheel:** every villa sold goes onto BSH management. One commission becomes recurring revenue

### The geography fork

Inventory says Ubud. The market says Canggu and Uluwatu.

| | Own Ubud | Attack the corridors | Both at once |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensibility | High. Near-monopoly on Magnitude resales | Low. 25 agencies already there | Split focus |
| Market size | Small pond, 4th by listing count | 75% of transactions | — |
| Uses the owner base | Fully | Barely | Partly |
| Team needed | Very small | Larger, or a very sharp niche | Larger |
| Time to first deal | Fast, warm stock | Slower | — |

**Recommendation: both, sequenced, not simultaneous.**

- **Months 1 to 4, own Ubud.** Stock exists, owners are warm, and nobody can out-inform BSH on a Magnitude unit. Fast cash, real deals, a track record to point at. Near-100% share of Magnitude resales is genuinely winnable
- **Month 4 onward, enter Canggu and Uluwatu on the underwriting position**, funded by Ubud revenue, with closed deals as proof. External listings, 2BR and 3BR, $250K to $600K

**Ubud is the beachhead, not the business.** The underwriting position is what travels to the corridors, and later to Jakarta.

## 8. Open questions, blocking

Answers to 1 and 4 could change the recommendation.

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Of the 538, how many are Magnitude/Ubud exactly, and what is the price and bedroom distribution? | 400 units of near-identical product is either a monopoly or a glut, depending on how many owners want out simultaneously |
