Status — Strategy overview
The brokerage starts from scratch, no sales track record today. But it already has something few independent brokerages in Bali have at this scale: 538 managed villas and the owners behind them. This sets out the strategy for turning that base into a brokerage business, and what it takes to resource it.
01 · The Opportunity
Why This Works
A standalone brokerage spends its first year earning trust and building a pipeline. BSH starts with a warm book of owners who already trust the company. The real opportunity: every managed villa is a potential brokerage lead, and every villa the brokerage sells is a potential new management contract.

03 · Positioning
Positioning & Differentiation
Scaling faster than anyone, on purpose:
  • Most of the 25+ agencies in Section 02 don't have real operational structure, founder-led, ad hoc, small. FazWaz is the exception, it already has structure, that's exactly why it's scaling the way it is.
  • In a market this fragmented, winning isn't a clever angle, it's out-structuring and out-scaling everyone else, building the systems (Section 06) faster than they can grow organically.
  • BSH's edge on FazWaz specifically isn't structure, it's being the more Bali-based, better-known local brand, with the owner network already in place.
  • Everything below, listings edge, warm-lead engine, naming, is in service of that speed, not separate ideas.
Framework Constraint-first · stage-based build (offer → fulfillment → sales → ops) · scale order: More → Better → New
Differentiation, corrected:
  • Oniriq, Bali Home Immo, Betterplace, Yolla already combine sales + management, not a new idea.
  • The edge is scale, 538 units, bigger than any of theirs (Oniriq's 350+ is closest).
  • Pitch: "biggest warm-lead engine in the market."
Best listings, not most listings:
  • 25+ agencies already (Section 02), no exclusivity, most overlap on the same inventory.
  • Inventory quality is the lever: owner-base access + a dedicated sourcing role (Section 06).
  • Secondary edge: real audited occupancy/revenue data across 538 villas, vs. competitors pitching projected yield.
Naming, the real issue:
  • Not about reputation risk crossing between the two businesses.
  • Bali Super Host is known as management, hard to also become known for sales under the same name.
  • Worth deciding whether the brokerage runs under its own identity rather than the same name.
Listing selectivity:
  • Start unselective, like every other agency, at launch.
  • Every listing passes through the Head of Brokerage for approval before going live.
  • Refine the accepted tier as we grow and see what actually sells.

05 · Go-To-Market
Four Revenue Channels
  • Priority order: sales training + lead gen come first, day one, that's what drives revenue.
  • The channels below and Section 06 resourcing exist to support that, not replace it.
01
Owner-Base Cross-Sell
Management into brokerage. Already know which owners show sell signals: falling occupancy, maintenance concerns, exit inquiries. Just need a process to act on it.
02
Developer & Land Partnerships
Priority: big developers with bigger projects, more units per deal. One large signing delivers a block of new management units in one motion, the fastest lever against the 750-unit target and the scaling thesis in Section 03. Feeds brokerage resale too, one pitch instead of two, the path to the 4 partnership agreements.
03
Outbound & Agent Network
Direct sourcing, marketing inbound, referral network, every hire trained on a structured process. The same approach proven over 5 years at Bali Exception, now backed by BSH's reach.
04
Post-Sale Handoff
Brokerage back into management. Every villa sold is offered BSH management first, one transaction becomes a long-term relationship.
  • Existing agency listings: stop listing managed villas with outside agencies once brokerage is live.
  • Where an outside agency already has an active buyer, propose a co-listing/co-broke split instead of pulling it cold.

  • All-in team cost: ~IDR 45-65M/month for the Year 1 hires (2 brokers, 2 listing agents, PA sourcer, admin), excludes marketing.
  • Size the budget conversation against this figure.
  • Systems: CRM + pipeline, a brokerage listing website connected to it, basic reporting. None of this exists yet, needs to be live before the team can produce.
  • Open: who owns the CRM-website integration day to day, not yet assigned.
Hiring profile: money-motivated, road-ready, no fear of the grind. Takes real time to find the right people, better to leave a seat open an extra month than fill it with the wrong one.
  • Team budget: the current model assumes IDR 15M/mo for a broker, IDR 25M/mo for a PA agent. Two brokers alone is IDR 30M, the entire stated budget, before anyone else exists.
  • Confirming real market rate is needed, with a fast approval path if the number moves.
Operating rhythm: weekly meetings per team (brokerage, PA/management, marketing), each focused on what needs to move that week, not a status report on everything.
  • Onboarding & training: its own structured workstream, a checklist tied to the sales funnel (Section 04).
  • Slow ramp in the first 90 days is the most expensive kind, it compounds against every KPI (Section 08).
  • Expect fast turnover early, that's normal, not a red flag. If someone's not landing it right away, train them before writing them off.
  • Hard cut points: not trainable, can't learn, cut at week 3. No deal closed, cut at month 2.
Framework Document → Duplicate → Certify · checklist-first, not tribal knowledge · grade on process, not outcome
Management Growth: Targets & Gaps
  • +212 units: split roughly 60/40 between Channel 02 (developer/land, bigger blocks) and Channel 01/03 (individual sourcing). Prioritize big developers with bigger projects inside that 60%, fewer, larger deals get to scale faster than many small ones (Section 03).
  • Gap: at the 3.0x/staff/month benchmark, one PA sourcer covers about a quarter of the ~12/month pace this needs.
  • Churn: 750 by Dec 2027 reads as pure addition, propose a 5-8%/year churn assumption so the real target is gross, not net.
  • Onboarding lag: track signed-to-live separately, 30-45 day target window, a signed deal sitting unlaunched isn't progress.
  • Contract terms: management fee %, length, exclusivity, not yet defined. Needed before a sourcer can offer owners anything.
Bali Super Legal — Proposed as Its Own Entity
  • Propose Legal & Corporate Services (PT PMA, visas, accounting) as a separate entity, not just the shared due-diligence resource above.
  • Self-funding: generates its own revenue, pays for its own costs, doesn't sit on the brokerage P&L.
  • Serves both sides: management contracts and brokerage sales both need it.
  • Precedent: Bali Exception and Bali Home Immo (Section 02) both already run this in-house.
07 · Execution
90-Day Roadmap
  • Days 1–30
    • Build the sales training program and lead generation engine
    • Build the marketing strategy with the marketing team before any ad spend
    • Hiring standards, SOPs for listing intake and commission/lead-routing rules
    • CRM and pipeline setup, decision on brokerage web presence
    • Pull the first owner sell-signal report from existing management data
    • Open the search for listing agents and PA sourcer (faster hires than senior brokers)
    • Confirm the legal resource-sharing arrangement, scope brokerage-specific training
  • Days 31–60
    • Marketing campaigns go live, reviewed and optimized as data comes in
    • Sales training runs with whoever's hired so far, lead generation live
    • PA sourcer starts first sourcing conversations toward the 4 partnership agreements
    • Legal (shared) trained on brokerage-specific due diligence, starts supporting deals
    • Senior broker search continues, no shortcut
  • Days 61–90
    • First pipeline review across whatever channels are live
    • Confirm commission and lead-routing rules between brokerage and PA
    • Finalize the handoff process with management
    • Recruit and adjust as the pipeline dictates, headcount isn't fixed at day 90

09 · Beyond Brokerage
Connecting Into Villa Management
A concrete way to stay connected to management without pulling focus: a monthly touchpoint with the management ops lead, routing owner sell-signals into brokerage (Channel 01), and routing brokerage clients who want to reinvest into a BSH-managed unit.
Time cost: roughly half a day a month, enough to keep the two businesses talking, not an operational role.
  • The incentive question: management is measured on retention, natural instinct to hold onto an owner rather than hand them to brokerage.
  • Not a reason to avoid this, it's the reason to bring it in-house, an owner who wants out sells one way or another.
  • Only real choice: through us, buyer offered BSH management first, or through an outside agent, losing both the sale and the contract.
CRM ownership: Admin & Listings Coordinator (Section 06) owns the brokerage CRM day to day. Management ops feeds it a monthly sell-signal export until real integration exists.
