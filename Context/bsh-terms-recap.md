# BSH / Kozystay: Full Terms Recap

**Status:** Terms negotiation, board approval pending. As of July 15, 2026. Not yet signed.

**Scenario framing for this recap:** assumes Matthieu is fully vested (Year 4, 125,000 shares) before any exit event happens. Two branches from there:
- **Scenario A:** Matthieu is still employed when Kozystay actually exits (Trade Sale or IPO) → 100% of vested shares pay out.
- **Scenario B:** Matthieu leaves before that exit happens (whether at Year 4 or any point after) → Good Leaver cap applies, 60% of vested shares pay out, 40% forfeited. (Leaving before Year 2 of the agreement, or as a Bad Leaver, pays out nil regardless of scenario, see Section 5.)

## 1 · Role & Reporting

- **Head of Brokerage (50%):** Build and run the brokerage from scratch. Full operational and commercial responsibility: team hiring, deal flow, marketing execution. No inherited structure.
- **Head of Property Acquisition (50%):** End-to-end unit acquisition: sourcing, qualification, negotiation, signing, handover to onboarding. Developer and property owner relationships.
- **Reporting:** Ian (MD) day-to-day. Dotted line to Frans (CCO, Kozystay Group) and Richard (Group Head of BD).
- **Scope boundary (confirmed July 10):** once a villa goes live, owner management hands fully to the existing owner relations team, which continues reporting to Ian, not Matthieu. Matthieu's ongoing footprint is developer and some-owner relationships, not day-to-day account management.
- **PA transition:** Richard continues co-managing the PA team short-term while Matthieu builds out brokerage. Full handover to Matthieu over the long run.
- **Decision authority:** Brokerage up to IDR 150M per transaction/initiative, PA up to IDR 50M per transaction, above those, Ian's approval. Reserved Matters (per Articles of Association) go to the Board/shareholders.

## 2 · Compensation

| Component | Terms |
|---|---|
| Base salary | IDR 90M gross/month + 1 month THR annually (~IDR 1,170M / ~$66.9K per year). No conditions, no quota. Annual review tied to KPI achievement. |
| Brokerage personal commission | 50% personal rate on brokerage deals, after an annual threshold. **2026: 2× of 50% gross annual salary = IDR 1,170M.** **2027 onward: 3× = IDR 1,755M.** Settled the month the threshold is crossed. |
| Brokerage profit share | **20%** of Brokerage Business Unit EBITDA (gross commissions minus marketing, salaries, direct overhead). |
| PA commission | 20/40% personal rate with quota, plus **15% overriding fee** on total PA team commissions. |

### What each line actually represents

- **Salary:** fixed, guaranteed pay. Paid regardless of production or team performance. The only figure in this recap not modeled or assumed, it's a flat contractual number.
- **Commission:** Matthieu's personal 50% cut of the deals *he* personally closes as a broker, paid once his annual threshold is crossed. This rewards individual production, separate from the team he's building.
- **Profit share:** Matthieu's 20% cut of the *whole brokerage unit's* profitability, gross commissions from personal and team sales combined, minus team costs, marketing, and his own salary and commission already paid out. This rewards him for running the unit, not just for closing his own deals.
- **PA override (added July 10, 2026):** Matthieu's 15% cut of the commission generated on management-fee revenue from every new unit the PA team brings under management. Unlike brokerage commission, this is earned on recurring monthly management fees, for as long as the villa stays under management, not a one-time payment at signing.

All at the same **$300K average sale price, 5% commission rate**. Commission is calculated only on Matthieu's own 12/year (× 50% personal rate). Profit share runs on the full total (all sales combined) through the EBITDA formula below, which is why profit share grows year over year while commission stays flat.

### Commission threshold basis (resolved July 15, 2026)

Matthieu proposed measuring the threshold against whole-department commission (his production + team's) rather than his personal production alone, so a heads-down hiring/training quarter doesn't cost him his rate. Frans declined the structural change but pointed out the existing structure already covers this:
- **20% profit share on Brokerage BU EBITDA is not subject to any quota** (see table above), so team-building time still pays out through that line even in a slow personal-production year.
- Over time, the profit share is expected to exceed the personal commission line anyway.
- **Flexibility offered instead:** co-execution with a junior sales staff member, splitting a deal's sales-quota credit 50:50 while the junior does most of the groundwork. Frans frames this as the standard model property consultants already use when splitting work with department heads.

Net effect: no change to the threshold figures or measurement basis above (still personal production, 2× / 3× gross salary). The 50:50 co-execution split is a new lever Matthieu can use on individual deals if he wants quota credit without doing the legwork himself.

### Assumptions behind the commission figure

- Matthieu personally closes **1 villa/month (12/year)**, average sale price **$300K**, at the standard Bali market rate of **5% seller-side commission**.
- His personal rate on that production is **50%**, once the annual threshold is crossed.
- Math: 12 villas × $300K × 5% × 50% = **$90,000/year**, every year of the model. This figure doesn't change between 2026 and 2027 despite the threshold shifting, because at his production pace the threshold is crossed well before year-end either way, and the full 50% rate applies retroactively to the whole year once crossed.
- **Threshold confirmed applied correctly:** 2026 threshold = 2× of 50% gross annual salary = IDR 1,170M, crossed by ~month 4-5. 2027 onward = 3× of 50% gross annual salary = IDR 1,755M, crossed by ~month 7. Both fall well inside the year, which is why the $90,000/year commission figure holds regardless of which multiplier applies in a given year.
- This is Matthieu's own historical Bali Exception pace carried forward, not a stretch assumption, it's the same production rate the whole model is built on.

### Assumptions behind the profit share figure

- Profit share is calculated on **Brokerage Business Unit EBITDA**: gross commissions from *all* brokerage sales (Matthieu's 12 personal villas plus the team's sales) minus team salaries and office (IDR 30M/month), marketing (IDR 40M/month), Matthieu's own brokerage-half salary, and Matthieu's own personal commission already paid out above.
- Team sales volume assumed: **24 villas in Year 1, 36 in Year 2, 48 in Years 3 and 4** (revised more conservative July 10, 2026, down from an initial 30/48/72/72), ramping as the hires in the 90-day roadmap land and reach production. Same $300K average price and 5% commission rate as the personal figure.
- This means the profit share grows year over year even though the personal commission figure stays flat, since it tracks team scale, not just Matthieu's own output.
- **The resulting EBITDA:** Year 1 ≈ IDR 6,450M ($369K), Year 2 ≈ IDR 9,600M ($549K), Years 3 to 4 ≈ IDR 12,750M ($729K) each. 20% of each of those is the profit share line below. Year 1 alone still comfortably clears the IDR 2.0B KPI target tied to ESOP vesting, even at this more conservative pace.

### Assumptions behind the PA override figure

- **Per-villa commission:** ~IDR 1.5M/month per newly-sourced unit under management, recurring for as long as it stays under management (confirmed by Matthieu, not a one-time signing bonus). Used directly as given, rather than re-derived from the 15%-of-gross-rental-revenue math, which would imply a higher figure.
- **Unit growth:** confirmed target is 538 → 750 units by Dec 31, 2027 (+212 in Year 1). No target exists yet for Years 2-4, this model assumes the same pace continues (+212 net new units/year), flagged as an unconfirmed placeholder.
- **Ramp:** units added evenly through each year, so the commission base averages the start- and end-of-year cumulative total, not the year-end figure. Only new units count, not the existing 538 base.
- **Override applied to the whole pool:** rather than splitting personal (20/40%) vs. team-sourced (15% override) commission, this model applies the 15% override to the entire new-unit pool, since there's no established personal PA sourcing-volume assumption to split against. Likely understates Matthieu's true take slightly.
- Result: IDR 286.2M (Year 1) growing to IDR 2,003.4M (Year 4) as the cumulative unit base compounds, IDR 4,579.2M ($262K) over 4 years.

This cash is earned regardless of what happens with the equity or with Kozystay's own exit. It's the number least dependent on anything outside Matthieu's control.

## 2b · Realistic Ramp Scenario (added 2026-08-07)

The table above assumes sales from month one and a full-time solo selling pace throughout. Two pieces of real-world evidence argue that's optimistic.

**The comparable:** a friend running a near-identical model, a brokerage bolted onto an already-established Bali villa management company (Alfred, founded 2018, 172 properties currently listed), had zero brokerage sales in 7 months. Root cause: Alfred feeds him listing leads, but no buyer leads. Same structural bet Kozystay's brokerage makes, an existing managed-unit base doesn't by itself produce buyer-side deal flow.

**Revised assumptions:**
- No sales through month 6. Personal production starts month 7. No closers hired before month 6, and hired closers need roughly 4 months before their first close, so team production doesn't start until month 10, then scales at the original pace with the same 9-month lag carried through, closing by Year 4.
- Realistic personal capacity while actually running Head of Brokerage and PA, not selling full-time: 4 deals in Year 1, rising to 6, 8, then 10 by Year 4 (versus the original flat 12/year).

**Where it bites hardest:** the personal commission threshold (3× of 50% gross annual salary, ~$100,286) is a fixed target, not a percentage of actual sales. At 4 and 6 deals/year, gross commission generated ($60,000 and $90,000) never crosses it, so **personal commission is $0 in both Year 1 and Year 2**, not prorated. It only starts paying in Year 3.

| Year | Salary | Commission | Profit Share | PA Override | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 (2027) | $67K | $0 | $15.4K | $16K | **$98.4K** |
| Y2 (2028) | $67K | $0 | $82.7K | $49K | **$198.7K** |
| Y3 (2029) | $67K | $60K | $112.7K | $82K | **$321.7K** |
| Y4 (2030) | $67K | $75K | $142.7K | $114K | **$398.7K** |
| **4-year total** | | | | | **$1,017.6K** |

Down from the original $1.364M, roughly a 25% cut. Recalculated Year 1 Brokerage EBITDA (~$47K) also lands well under the ~$114K (IDR 2.0B) target gating that year's ESOP vesting, a real risk of missing Year 1 equity vesting from ramp timing alone, not underperformance. Worth raising with Frans directly: whether the Year 1 KPI accounts for a realistic hiring ramp, and whether the commission threshold should scale with a reduced personal pace rather than staying a fixed target.

There's no way to "wait it out" from outside the company: once you've left, the 40% is gone regardless of how long you wait for the exit to eventually happen.
